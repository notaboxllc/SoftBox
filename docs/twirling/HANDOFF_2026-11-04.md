# Handoff 2026-11-04 — low-ATP paired twirl campaign (LOWATP_PAIR)

This document lets a new session pick up the twirl work and run the parked campaign without other context. It
supersedes `HANDOFF_2026-10-12.md` for the twirl campaign. Read CLAUDE.md "MODEL CANON" and `docs/MODEL_CANON.md` first.

## 1. Bottom line

- **The model** is canon v5 on `main` (pushed 2026-11-04; branch `gpu-mat-bottlenecks-explicit-singlehead` matches).
- **The twirl** is RIGHT-handed in every run so far, at saturating ATP and at 20 µM.
  - **At high ATP that matches experiment in sign.** Nishizaka 1993 (HMM, 1–2 mM MgATP, supercoiling assay) found a
    right-handed torque.
  - **At low ATP it does not.** Beausang 2008 (5–20 µM) found myosin II twirling LEFT-handed, pitch 0.47 µm, about
    **+2.1 turns/µm** in our convention, in about 20 % of filaments.
  - Both Beausang (rigor drag) and Vilfan 2009 (target-zone binding bias) propose an ATP-dependent crossover: a
    right-handed stroke dominates at high ATP and a left-handed effect at low ATP. See §7.
- **Our low-ATP data have not crossed over yet,** but they are only about 1–1.5σ on a single lawn. The structural
  ψ = +4° gives a right-handed swing, and Beausang argued that the stroke path alone cannot give the left-handed
  low-ATP twirl.
- **Low ATP is therefore the discriminating condition,** and ψ = 0 isolates whatever left-handed mechanism the model has
  (helical site lattice and rigor drag).
- **jba's gold standard** (verbatim): "a multi-second run (multiple runs) demonstrating a rotation that experimentalists
  would measure". Do not offer roll torque or other short-run proxies as a substitute.
- **The next step** is the parked campaign `LOWATP_PAIR`:
  - 20 µM ATP, d400, 3 s per run;
  - ψ = +4° (canon) vs ψ = 0, paired on lawns 02, 03 and 04.
  - Approved by jba ("run the pair at d400"). jba is travelling, so it must run unattended.

## 2. Conventions you need

- **Sign of twirl.** Negative turns/µm = RIGHT-handed. "+roll" = left-handed.
- **Height.**
  - The summary line `FILAMENT HEIGHT` is the axis height above the lawn.
  - The trajectory column `z_now_nm` is ABSOLUTE centre z. The lawn is about 7 nm below z = 0, so add about 7 nm.
    (I once misreported 15 nm because of this; the real value was 22 nm.)
  - Floor and ceiling contact use the lowest and highest point anywhere on the filament, so a lifted end counts.
  - The trajectory columns `zmin`/`zmax` are running extremes, not the current height.
- **Cost.**
  - 8M steps per simulated second (dt 1.25e-7 s).
  - About 12 ms/step with one JVM running, about 20 ms/step with three.
  - A 3 s run (24M steps) at three concurrent JVMs takes about 5.6 days.
- **Thermal roll noise.**
  - At low ATP the effective roll diffusion is about 5–15 rad²/s. Spread = sqrt(2·D·T): about 0.4–0.6 turns at 0.5 s,
    about 0.9–1.5 turns at 3 s.
  - A −2.3 turns/µm twirl at 0.5 µm/s over 3 s is about −3.4 turns, so roughly 2.4σ per lawn and about 4σ pooled over
    three lawns.
- **Existence gate.** A twirl claim needs independent lawns (seeds 02, 03, 04, …). Runs on one lawn at different
  densities or ceilings are NOT independent. Report resolution before effect size.

### 4a. The unattended queue: `scripts/park_queue.sh <campaign_dir>`

Tested with a fake launcher: fresh start, crash, resume, queue restart and done.

- **Polling.** It checks every `POLL` = 300 s.
- **Done.** A job is DONE when `<tag>/summary.txt` exists. That file is written only at the end of a run.
  - **Caveat:** a run that hits a stop criterion (`-stop-offlawn 0.1`, `-target 16.0` from `canon_glide.sh`) also writes
    a summary, so it counts as DONE early. Check `STOP=` in each summary.
- **Concurrency.** At most `MAXJ` = 3 SiteNormal JVMs on the whole machine, counting runs the queue did not start.
- **Recorder.** It will not launch unless `gpu-crash-recorder` is RUNNING; it logs the refusal instead.
- **Crash recovery.** If a job's launcher dies with no summary and `checkpoint.bin` exists:
  1. It moves the segment outputs (trajectory, episodes, progress, tether, milestones, run_config, run log) into
     `<tag>/seg_NN/`.
  2. It copies `checkpoint.bin` to `seg_NN/checkpoint_at_resume.bin`.
  3. It relaunches the job with `-resume`.
- **Limits.** After `MAXR` = 6 launches of one job it gives up and writes `<tag>.gaveup`. It never kills anything.
- **Viewer link.** It points `~/Code/SoftBox/threejs_<tag>` at the job's frames.
- **Logs.** The queue logs to `park.log`. Each job writes `<tag>.log`, `<tag>.pid` and `<tag>.launches`.
- **Overrides.** Environment variables `LAUNCHER`, `POLL`, `MAXJ`, `MAXR` and `GAP` (seconds between launches,
  default 60).
- **Hard GPU freeze.** After a freeze and reboot the queue does not restart, by design. Per CLAUDE.md, run
  `./scripts/collect_gpu_crash_case.sh` first, then restart the queue. It resumes each job from its checkpoint, which is
  written every 25k steps.

## 7. Literature for the swing tilt (ψ), stroke skew (ε) and twirl handedness

The full annotated list (50 refs) is in `docs/twirl/ACTIN_SITE_LATTICE_LITERATURE_BASIS.md` §References. The
measurement of ψ on the structures (8R9V → 8RBF) is in that document and in `docs/MODEL_CANON.md` (v3 and v4 entries).

**Swing tilt sign and size (structure): canon v3 and v4**
- **Klebl DP, et al. (2025). *Nature* 642:519–526. PMID 40205053.**
  - Time-resolved cryo-EM of actomyosin-5a; PDB 8R9V (ADP·Pi, primed) and 8RBF (ADP).
  - The lever swings about 93°, "displaced azimuthally by only 4° right-handed".
  - Our superposition gives ψ = +4.5–6.4° (lever helix) or about 2° (converter rotation axis), right-handed.
  - This is the basis for ψ = +4°.
- **Beausang JF, Schroeder HW, Nelson PC, Goldman YE (2008). *Biophys J* 95:5820** (arXiv 0810.3925).
  - Polarized-TIRF twirl in a modified gliding assay, at 5–20 µM ATP (the reason for our 20 µM).
  - Myosin II twirls LEFT-handed with pitch 0.47 ± 0.19 µm; myosin V LEFT-handed with pitch 1.7 µm.
  - The docked myosin II structures move Lys843 with a "slight (~20º) right-handed tilt", so "a tilt in the other
    direction would be required"; the twirl "is not explained by the path of the power stroke". Coupling between linear
    and angular velocity is weak.
  - Their candidate mechanisms are drag from rigor heads and step size relative to the actin helix. These motivate the
    ψ = 0 arm.
  - This paper is the experimental target and the source of the sign conflict.

**Stroke skew ε (retired in canon v3)**
- Conserved-core contacts barely move between 8R9V and 8RBF (−0.2°), from the Klebl structures. Analysis in
  `ACTIN_SITE_LATTICE_LITERATURE_BASIS.md`.
- The interface grows asymmetrically (375 → 729 Å², Klebl's numbers), which explains an apparent +5.4° as an artifact.

**Other twirl and rotation experiments** (numbering as in the basis doc)
- [35] Tanaka Y, Ishijima A, Ishiwata S (1992). *BBA* 1159:94–98. Supercoiling of actin in a motility assay.
- [36] Nishizaka T, Yagi T, Tanaka Y, Ishiwata S (1993). "Right-handed rotation of an actin filament in an in vitro
  motile system." *Nature* 361:269–271. doi 10.1038/361269a0, PMID 8423853.
  - **Read 2026-11-04:** abstract (Europe PMC) plus how Beausang 2008 and Vilfan 2009 discuss it. The full text is
    paywalled; requested in `docs/literature/PAYWALLED_REQUESTS.md`.
  - **Assay:** the front of the filament is fixed to the glass by cross-linked HMM, and the rear slides on a track of
    HMM. The filament supercoils. A left-handed single turn of superhelix forms just before supercoiling, which implies
    the sliding force "has a right-handed torque component that induces the right-handed rotation of an actin filament
    around its long axis".
  - **Conditions** (from Beausang; the full text is unread): HMM at high MgATP. Beausang gives "1 mM" in one place and
    "2 mM" in another. The read-out gives a sign only, not a pitch.
  - **Consequence for us:** at saturating ATP the experimental sign is RIGHT-handed, and so is ours (canon v4: −1.67,
    −1.07 turns/µm). Our saturating result therefore agrees in sign with the only high-ATP experiment. The conflict
    with experiment is only at LOW ATP (Beausang, left-handed).
  - **The papers propose an ATP-dependent sign crossover:**
    - Beausang (rigor-drag model, their Fig. 8B): the power stroke is slightly right-handed. Rigor heads drag backward
      and slightly sideways, which gives left-handed twirl at low ATP. Rigor heads are fewer at high ATP, so the
      left-handed tendency falls away, "and thus possibly reconcile" the two results.
    - Vilfan 2009, Discussion: preferential binding before the target-zone centre gives left-handed twirl, largest at
      speeds below 100 nm/s. If the stroke has the opposite helicity, "there could be a cross-over from right handed
      motion under high ATP concentrations, to left-handed under low".
    - **Our model contains both ingredients:** the right-handed stroke (ψ +4°) and a helical every-4 site lattice. The
      ψ = 0 arm isolates the second, plus whatever rigor drag the model produces.
- Beausang details relevant to the campaign (arXiv 0810.3925, full text read):
  - The rabbit skeletal myosin II gliding assay ran at 5–20 µM MgATP, velocities 0.1–0.5 µm/s. Polarization data were
    hard to get above about 0.25 µm/s, so most data are slower.
  - Only about 20 % of analysable filaments twirled: 97 twirlers out of roughly 550–650. Of those, 94 were left-handed
    and 3 right-handed. Pitch −0.47 ± 0.2 µm (SD, n = 94).
  - Angular and linear velocity are correlated, but the fits do not pass through zero; they read this as kinetics, not
    structure alone.
  - So "experiment" is a left-handed subpopulation at low speed. Our 20 µM runs glide at about 0.5 µm/s, at the top of
    their range.
